Everyday Wellbeing Guide (sample document)

This short guide was written for the demo project. It contains general educational information only. It is not medical advice and it is not a substitute for a doctor, psychologist, or counsellor.

1. Understanding stress
Stress is the body's natural reaction to pressure. In short bursts it can help us focus, but long periods of stress can leave a person tired, irritable, or unable to concentrate. Common signs include tense muscles, headaches, trouble sleeping, and feeling overwhelmed. Noticing these signs early makes it easier to respond with small, healthy changes.

2. Sleep habits
Regular sleep supports mood and concentration. Helpful habits include going to bed and waking up at similar times every day, keeping the bedroom dark, quiet, and cool, and avoiding screens for about an hour before bed. Caffeine late in the day can make it harder to fall asleep. If sleep problems last for several weeks, it is a good idea to speak with a healthcare professional.

3. Slow breathing exercise
Slow breathing can help the body settle when feeling tense. Sit comfortably. Breathe in gently through the nose for about four seconds, then breathe out slowly through the mouth for about six seconds. Repeat for two to five minutes. If you feel dizzy, stop and breathe normally.

4. Small daily routines
Small routines are easier to keep than big plans. Examples include a ten minute walk, drinking water regularly, eating meals at regular times, and spending a few minutes outdoors in daylight. Physical movement is linked with better mood and lower stress for many people.

5. Staying connected
Talking with friends, family, or classmates can reduce the feeling of being alone with a problem. You do not need to have the perfect words. Saying "I've been having a hard time lately" is a good start. Sharing a worry with someone you trust often makes it feel lighter.

6. Managing study or work pressure
Break big tasks into small steps, and plan short breaks. Work for about 25 to 45 minutes, then rest for five minutes. Write down worries so they are not all held in the mind. Setting realistic goals for each day helps avoid feeling behind.

7. When to seek professional help
Consider contacting a doctor, psychologist, or counsellor when low mood, worry, or stress lasts for weeks, makes daily life difficult, or affects school, work, or relationships. Asking for help is a sign of strength. If someone is in immediate danger, contact local emergency services right away.
